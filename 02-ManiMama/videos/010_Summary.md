1. **Definitions**  
   - **Punyagati** (punya‑gati): *Karma* performed with the *goal of accumulating merit (punya)* and a desire for its favorable results or personal benefit. This is “drishti” – motivated by outcome, attachment to reward, and ego.
   - **Sankhyagati** (sanktyaga): *Karma* performed without any *desire for result*; purely with the intention of attaining *chitta‑shuddhi* (purification of mind) or liberation. This is “adhriṣhti” – selfless, detached, and aligned with moksha.

2. **Motivation**  
   - **Punyagati**: Driven by desire for merit, fame, material gain, or personal satisfaction (“kama” in the sense of ego‑fulfillment). The performer expects a favorable result later (future benefit).
   - **Sankhyagati**: Motivated solely by *purification* and liberation; no expectation of any reward. The action is performed as an offering to the Self.

3. **Consequences**  
   - **Punyagati**: Generates merit that can be accumulated over time but may also accumulate karmic bondage if not balanced with release (e.g., through renunciation, bhakti). It tends toward *attachment* and later *bondage*.
   - **Sankhyagati**: Leads directly to liberation because the mind becomes purified; it neutralizes both *punya* and *apa* (sin) karmas simultaneously. The result is freedom from samsara.

4. **How to Identify / Practice Sanktyaga**  
   1. **Pure Intent**: Perform the action with the clear intention: “I do this for my own purification, not for any personal gain.”  
   2. **Detachment**: Avoid thinking, “If I perform well, I’ll receive a reward later.” Let go of expectations completely.  
   3. **Renunciation Mindset**: Treat the act as an offering to the Divine or Self, akin to chanting *“योगे निरस्तादृति”* (renouncing attachment).  
   4. **Remembrance of Death‑Like State**: Keep a mind that constantly reminds you: “Even if I were dead now, I would still perform this duty for the sake of purity.” This reinforces non‑attachment.

5. **Relation to Yoga**  
   - Both types are *karma* yoga, but Sanktyaga aligns with *jnanayoga* (knowledge path) because it aims at *chitta‑shuddhi*. In contrast, Punyagati often aligns more with *bhakti* or *materialistic* desires.
   - The ultimate goal of both is liberation (*moksha*), but the pathway differs: Punyagati may lead to *karmic bondage* if not balanced by renunciation; Sanktyaga naturally leads to *nirvana*.

6. **Practical Example**  
   - **Punyagati**: A person offers a puja expecting blessings for health and wealth (“I will be rewarded later”). The motive is self‑interest.
   - **Sankhyagati**: A devotee performs the same puja only to purify his mind, without any expectation of material or social benefit. His sole purpose is internal purification.

7. **Why It Matters**  
   - Practicing Sanktyaga reduces *sanchita* (accumulated) karmas because it counteracts both positive and negative karma simultaneously through the power of renunciation.  
   - It also cultivates inner peace, which is essential for sustained spiritual progress and prevents relapse into ego‑driven activities.
