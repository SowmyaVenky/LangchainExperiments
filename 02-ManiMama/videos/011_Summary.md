1. **What Shri Shankara says about karma‑yoga and liberation**  
   - In the classic “Shruti” (especially in the *Brahma Sutra* 5.2.33) and his own commentary (*Bhāgavata Pūrṇa* 3.6.21), Shri Shankara teaches that karma‑yoga is a means—**not an end**.  
   - The ultimate goal of all yoga (including karma‑yoga) is realization of the Self (*Ātman*) and thereby liberation (*mokṣa*).  
   - Because karma‑yoga deals only with action without attachment, it removes the bond between samsāra and the individual consciousness. Once this bondage is removed, true mokṣa follows automatically.

2. **Why karma‑yoga does not give liberation directly**  
   - **Temporary vs. permanent change:** Karma‑yoga purifies the mind’s attachment to results; it eliminates the *aversion* (avidyā) that fuels samsāra, but the deeper identification with a separate self (*Ishvara*) remains until one fully realizes that the Self is Brahman.  
   - **Stages of liberation:** Shankara enumerates three stages:  
     1. **Jñāna‑viveka** – discrimination between true and false (the “self‑realization” stage).  
     2. **Mokṣa** – actual release from the cycle of birth/death, which is realized only after one sees that *Ātman = Brahman*.  
   - Therefore, karma‑yoga prepares the practitioner for liberation but does not instantly confer mokṣa.

3. **Kṛṣṇa’s clarification (as presented in the text)**  
   - In the quoted passage, Kṛṣṇa explicitly says: *“Karma‑yoga does not directly lead to liberation; it merely removes attachment.”*  
   - This aligns with Shankara’s doctrine that **karma‑yoga is a preparatory path**, while true mokṣa comes from deeper insight (*jñāna*) and realization of the identity between individual consciousness (*Ātman*) and universal consciousness (*Brahman*).

4. **Practical implication**  
   - Practitioners should view karma‑yoga as a **gateway**: by performing selfless actions (without expectation), one gradually cultivates detachment, humility, and wisdom—conditions that enable eventual realization of mokṣa through deeper study of the *Shruti* and subsequent stages of yoga (jñāna‑yoga).
